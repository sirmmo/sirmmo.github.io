Thesis

For thirty years, lock-in was just math: connecting two systems was expensive, so walling off your data paid. Large language models quietly broke that math — they're not the engine that does the work but the lever that makes connection nearly free, and once connection is cheap the walled garden flips from the safe bet to the expensive mistake. The question is no longer whether you can afford to connect; it's whether you can afford not to.

§ II

Games, playable

  1. City simulation

    citysim

    OpenCity, the GPL city builder, ported to Rust and played in the browser. It can rebuild a city from GeoJSON, so the maps of Open History Map and Open Fantasy Maps become places you can zone and grow.

    Rust · WebAssembly · GeoJSON ▸ Play ↗ Repo
  2. Turn-based tactics

    OpenTactics

    Skirmishes on a square grid: capture cities, build units, take the HQ. The rules are one JSON file and every army plays by the same ones. Against the computer, on one screen, or browser to browser for up to 16 players.

    Rust · WebAssembly · WebRTC ▸ Play ↗ Repo
  3. Factory building

    OpenFactor

    A factory-building platform on a deterministic simulation: no clock, no floats, no randomness, so saves, replays and co-op sync come for free. An OpenTactics factory can be handed to a friend to run here.

    Rust · WebAssembly · WebRTC ▸ Play ↗ Repo
  4. 3D platformer

    Capitan Pachino 64

    A tomato pirate goes home to the Garden Isle for 27 Golden Stars across five worlds. Vegetables are friends; steaks and fish are not.

    JavaScript · three.js ▸ Play ↗ Repo
